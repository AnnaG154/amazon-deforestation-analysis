# 📖 Amazonia: Historia z danych (Data Storytelling)

🎬 Opening Question:
**WHAT DRIVES THE DESTRUCTION OF THE AMAZON?**

---

## 🗺️ Section 1: What the numbers reveal

Between 2004 and 2019, the Amazon rainforest lost over **160,000 km²** of forest — an area almost the size of the United Kingdom.

But this destruction wasn't evenly spread. One Brazilian state — **Pará** — accounted for more than **62,000 km²** of forest loss. That’s over **39,0%** of the total deforestation in the Legal Amazon, concentrated in just one region.

A closer look reveals a disturbing trend: while some states showed moderate deforestation, others saw **sharp spikes in specific years**. What drove those spikes?

---

## 📊 Section 2: A dual view – zooming into space and time

The chart below tells two stories at once:

- The **bars** represent deforestation per state per year — where and how much forest was cleared.
- The **line** shows the overall trend for the entire Legal Amazon — rising, falling, rising again.

Together, they highlight not just **who cut the forest**, but also **when the Amazon bled most severely**.

➡️ In 2004 alone, deforestation exceeded **27,000 km²** — the worst year of the entire period.

---

## 🔮 What next?

Our next step is to look beyond the forest — to the **climate patterns** that may have amplified or triggered these losses.

➡️ Do extreme weather events like **El Niño** play a hidden role?

## 🌦️ Section 3: When the sky turns against the forest

The Amazon doesn't only suffer from chainsaws and bulldozers — **climate patterns** play a powerful role too.

During years marked by **El Niño** and **La Niña**, deforestation rates surged. These climate phenomena don’t directly cut trees — but they shape the conditions that make forest loss easier and more likely.

- **El Niño** years often bring droughts, drying the forest and making it more vulnerable to fire.
- Surprisingly, **La Niña** years — typically wetter — also correlate with high deforestation, possibly because they **encourage agricultural expansion** once the rains have passed.

➡️ In both cases, **extreme climate amplifies human impact**.

---

## 🔥 Section 4: Fire — the most intentional tool

Fires in the Amazon are rarely natural.

When we map fire hotspots using geographic coordinates, they **cluster in areas with the highest deforestation**.  
Zoom in, and a pattern emerges: fire is often **used to clear land**, especially after selective logging or illegal encroachment.

This map doesn’t just show damage — it shows **strategy**.

---

## 📆 Section 5: The fire season — short, deadly, predictable

Fire isn’t spread evenly across the calendar.

Month by month, data reveals a pattern: **August to October** is fire season. Every year, like clockwork, the flames rise as the dry season peaks.

This isn’t a coincidence — it’s a calendar of destruction.

➡️ Targeted enforcement and prevention must focus on these **high-risk months**, when intentional burning reaches its peak.

---

## 🧠 Final Insight: It's not one problem — it's all of them at once

Deforestation in the Amazon is a **synergistic crisis**:

- Climate extremes,
- Economic pressure for land,
- Weak or poorly enforced environmental policy,
- And the use of fire as a cheap, fast land-clearing tool.

Each factor alone is dangerous.  
**Together, they’re deadly.**

---

> 🌍 The Amazon is not simply disappearing.  
> It is being systematically dismantled — piece by piece, fire by fire, year by year.
